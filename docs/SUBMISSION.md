## Title
HeatShield — heat early warning that tells you what to do, in your language

## One-line pitch
A weather app tells you it's 41 °C; HeatShield tells an outdoor worker, an older adult or a parent
what to do about it in the next few hours, in their own language, and warns them before the
hottest hours arrive.

## The problem
Heat is linked to an estimated 489,000 deaths a year (WHO, 2000–2019 average). 2.4 billion of the
world's 3.4 billion workers are likely to be exposed to excessive heat at work, and heat causes an
estimated 18,970 work-related deaths a year (ILO, April 2024). The people most at risk (older
adults, people with chronic illness, outdoor workers, families without cooling) are the least
likely to get a warning that is specific to their body, their job and their language. A forecast
gives a number. Nobody turns it into "stop heavy work from 13:00 to 17:00, drink a cup of water
every 15–20 minutes, and here are the signs that mean call for help" — in Urdu, Arabic or Hindi.

## What we built
- **Real heat science, per person:** live hourly forecast (Open-Meteo) → the US National Weather
  Service heat-index formula (verified against an independent implementation) → risk tier hour by
  hour, with the trend, the 24-hour peak, the person's risky hours ("now until 04:00 tomorrow") and
  a 4-day outlook.
- **Personal thresholds:** older adults, pregnant people, young children and people with chronic
  conditions are warned one tier earlier than the general public.
- **An action plan in 13 languages, generated by Amazon Bedrock** (Claude Haiku 4.5, with Amazon
  Nova 2 Lite as automatic fallback): a headline, three concrete steps with clock times, and the
  warning signs to act on — grounded in CDC/NIOSH guidance, right-to-left for Arabic and Urdu.
- **Alerts people don't have to ask for:** EventBridge Scheduler re-checks everyone hourly; Amazon
  SNS emails people when their risk reaches their level (one alert per level per day, never at
  night).
- **The Community lane multiplier:** a foreman, teacher or clinic worker creates a group, shares
  one invite link, and watches everyone's live risk on one private dashboard, worst first.

## Technical innovation (AWS)
- **Amazon Bedrock, engineered for a public endpoint.** The prompt is built only from enumerated
  values (tiers, clock hours, profile, language) — no user text ever reaches the model, which
  removes prompt injection and makes the prompt space finite. That lets us cache guidance in
  DynamoDB under a hash of the exact inputs, so spend is bounded and the same crew gets the same
  advice from one call. Output is validated (JSON shape, length, and Unicode script) and falls
  back Claude → Nova → pre-written guidance, so a person at risk never sees a blank screen. This
  chain carried the app through launch day, when the new account's Claude access was pending.
- **Fully serverless, one SAM template:** CloudFront (Origin Access Control, strict CSP/HSTS) +
  private S3, API Gateway HTTP API with per-route throttling, three least-privilege Lambda
  functions on Graviton, four on-demand DynamoDB tables (sparse GSI, TTL, atomic conditional-write
  alert dedupe), SNS with per-subscriber filter policies, EventBridge Scheduler, X-Ray.
- **Privacy by design:** locations rounded to ~1 km; emails never stored in our database (only in
  the SNS subscription, double opt-in); bearer secrets stored as SHA-256; one-click delete.

## How the coding agent (Claude Code) helped ship it
Claude Code was connected to AWS through the official AWS Agent Toolkit (browser `aws login`, then
the AWS MCP Server — `aws-mcp ✔ Connected`), and the full process is logged with real command
output in `docs/BUILD_LOG.md`. Examples:
1. It took the Bedrock model IDs from a real `ListInferenceProfiles` call through the AWS MCP
   server rather than from memory.
2. It verified the heat-index code against MetPy reference values and pinned them in tests (48
   unit tests).
3. It drove a real browser through the live site after every deploy; screenshots exposed an
   ambiguous "Risky 14:00–13:00" label and a stray "null", both fixed the same day.
4. It read the Lambda logs to discover that Claude access needed Anthropic's use-case form, and
   that Nova's Bengali/Swahili output had word errors — documented honestly as a limitation.
5. It found an abuse vector in its own code (sign-ups trigger AWS confirmation emails) and
   throttled those routes before launch.

## Impact and path to scale
HeatShield is free and works anywhere Open-Meteo covers (the whole planet). One leader can protect
up to 200 people per group today; the serverless design costs almost nothing when idle and scales
with use. Natural adopters: construction and farm crews, delivery fleets, senior-living and
home-care networks, schools, and community health workers. Next steps: SMS/WhatsApp delivery via
AWS End User Messaging, fluent-speaker review of each language, and WBGT for occupational use.

## Honest limitations
Heat index is a shade value (we say so on screen). AI guidance passes automated structure and
script checks; meaning in lower-resource languages still needs fluent review. Email is the live
alert channel; SMS is not claimed. HeatShield gives safety information, not medical care.
